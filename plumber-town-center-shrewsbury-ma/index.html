The Town Center at a Glance

These anchor points describe where the Town Center sits. They are not a boundary map, because we found no official boundary line.

  • Common: laid out in 1721 at the corner of what are now Main and Prospect Streets, per the Shrewsbury Historic District entry.
  • Historic district: about 14 acres around the Common, declared locally in 1972 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places on October 8, 1976.
  • Landmarks: the First Congregational Church (1766), the Shrewsbury Public Library (1903) at Main and Grafton Streets and an 1830 brick schoolhouse that now holds town offices.
  • Study area: a 2019 Town Center study describes the center as roughly defined by Prospect and Boylston Streets to the north, St. Mary's Church to the east and the Post Office to the south and west.
  • Zoning: Limited Business, according to that same study.

A Mixed Area Means Mixed Calls

The historic district lists civic buildings, a church and a few early 19th century houses near Boylston and Grafton Streets. The 2019 study also pictures shops, offices, services and restaurants on ground floors, with housing needed to make redevelopment pay.

That mix means more than one kind of plumbing visit. A house may need a kitchen drain cleared or a water heater looked at. A shop or office may need a restroom leak traced or a grease line cleaned, which is where commercial plumbing and hydro jetting come in.

Older and Renovated Buildings: Look Before You Assume

A building's age does not tell you what pipe is inside its walls. Pipe material depends on when the work was done and what has been replaced since, and buildings renovated several times can hold more than one material.

The dependable way to know is to look. A plumber can check exposed supply pipes in the basement, and a sewer camera inspection shows the real condition of a drain line without digging. Plumbing work in Massachusetts falls under the state plumbing standards in 248 CMR 10.00.
