Disposal Jammed or Leaking: Start Here

Turn the disposal switch off and, for a jam, unplug it or switch off its breaker before doing anything else. Never put your hand inside the chamber. If water is leaking into the cabinet, close the valves under the sink or the main valve and call the 24/7 line, and remember that a clogged drain will keep the sink filling while you wait.

Reading a Disposal Problem

A disposal has only a few ways to fail, and the leak location tells most of the story.

When a Slow Sink Is Not the Disposal

If the disposal runs fine but the sink drains slowly or fills with gray water, the clog is in the drain line. Fat, coffee grounds and starchy food build up in the pipe behind the disposal, which is a job for drain cleaning rather than a new unit. If the whole kitchen line is coated with grease, hydro jetting may be the longer-lasting fix.

Replacing or Installing a Disposal

A disposal is worth replacing when the housing leaks, the motor is burned out or it jams constantly. Replacement units come in a range of horsepower ratings, and a larger motor handles tougher scraps. We disconnect the old unit, set the new one on the sink flange, reconnect the drain and dishwasher hose and test for leaks.

If your kitchen has never had a disposal, installation also needs an electrical connection and a knockout plug removed on the dishwasher port. Tell the dispatcher what you are planning, and if the sink is also being replaced, see plumbing installation.

Disposal Habits That Prevent Calls

A few habits keep a disposal running for years.

  • Run cold water while grinding and for a few seconds after: it carries waste past the trap.
  • Skip grease, coffee grounds and fibrous scraps: celery, corn husks and potato peels wrap and clog.
  • Avoid hard items like bones and fruit pits: they jam the impellers.
  • Do not use chemical cleaners: they damage seals and endanger the next plumber.
  • Clean with ice and citrus peels now and then: it scrubs the chamber without harm.

Older Shrewsbury Kitchens

Kitchens in older houses may have cast iron or galvanized drain lines behind the sink, where rough scale catches food waste. A new disposal on a scaled line clogs just as the old one did, so we check the line when we replace a unit. Kitchens in Westborough and the other nearby towns get the same check.

What to Do Before You Reach Into a Disposal

Do not put your hand in the disposal chamber, even when it seems off. Switch off the power at the breaker, then use a hex key or tongs. Do not pour chemical cleaners into a jammed unit.

Disposal connections to the sink drain follow the state plumbing standards in 248 CMR 10.00.
