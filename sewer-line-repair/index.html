When a Sewer Line Failure Is an Emergency

A sewer line that has collapsed or cracked can send waste into the yard or back into the house. If sewage is surfacing or coming up a floor drain, call the 24/7 line, stop water use throughout the house and keep everyone away from the wet area. The first visit clears the line enough to stop the backup with sewer line cleaning, then the repair is planned from what the camera shows.

What Failure Looks Like

A sewer line fails slowly, then suddenly. Early signs are repeated clogs, a smell near the foundation or a patch of lawn that stays green and wet. By the time sewage backs up, the pipe may already have a break, an offset joint or a sag where water pools and catches solids.

A camera inspection is the only reliable way to tell a root clog from a damaged pipe. Cutting roots out of a cracked pipe clears it for a while, and the roots return along with the clog.

Repair Options for a Damaged Sewer Line

The right repair depends on what the camera shows, not on a standard recommendation.

MethodBest forTrade-off
Spot repair (dig and replace a section)One broken joint or crack at a known pointDigs one hole, but the rest of the old pipe stays
Trenchless pipe liningLong runs with cracks or root entry, sound structure otherwiseLess digging, but the pipe must be clean and round first
Pipe burstingOld pipe that is too damaged to linePulls new pipe through the old path with small access pits
Full replacement by trenchCollapsed, offset or badly bellied pipeMost disruptive, and the most complete fix

Pipe lining is a liner cured inside the old pipe to form a new pipe within it. It needs the line to be cleaned first, often by sewer line cleaning or jetting.

Shrewsbury Houses and Their Laterals

Houses from the early and middle 1900s may have clay, cast iron or fiber pipe in the lateral, and those materials crack and shift over decades. Newer houses have PVC, which resists roots but can sag if the trench settled. Shrewsbury's mix of old and new streets means the pipe in your yard could be either, and the same question comes up in Northborough and Grafton.

Yard work and driveways add to the complication. A line under pavement or near a large tree changes which repair makes sense, and a trenchless method may avoid tearing up landscaping.

Permits and Inspection

Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR, requires a permit from the plumbing inspector before most plumbing work starts. The plumber applies for it and the inspector checks the finished work. Rules for work in the public street or at the town connection can differ, so ask the Shrewsbury building department or the town's public works office before you dig.

What to Do When the Sewer Backs Up

Stop using water, keep children and pets away from the affected area and do not run the washer or dishwasher. Do not use chemical cleaners in a backed-up line.
