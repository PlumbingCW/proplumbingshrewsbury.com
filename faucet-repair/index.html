Valve Will Not Shut Off? Do This First

If a faucet will not stop or a supply line is spraying, close the small valve under the sink by turning it clockwise. If that valve will not turn or is leaking, shut the main valve for the house and call the 24/7 line. Place towels and a bucket in the cabinet and keep your hands clear of water near electrical outlets.

A dripping faucet is not an emergency, but a failed shutoff valve behind a fixture is, because you cannot stop the water to repair it. That is why we replace worn valves while we are in the cabinet.

Cartridges, Brands and Hard-to-Find Parts

Most modern single-handle faucets from Moen, Delta, Grohe, Kohler and others use a replaceable cartridge, and the part number matters. If you know the brand and model, tell the dispatcher so the plumber can bring the cartridge. When the model is discontinued, we say so and recommend a replacement faucet instead of chasing a part.

Shutoff Valves and Supply Lines

The small valves under sinks and behind toilets are called angle stops, and they are often forgotten until they fail. An old valve that will not close or leaks from the stem needs to be replaced so you can stop the water in a real emergency. Braided supply lines that are cracked or bulging can also burst, so we replace them as part of the repair.

In older Shrewsbury houses, the valves may be decades old and corroded in place. It is worth replacing them while someone is already working under the sink.

Showers, Tubs and Other Fixtures

A dripping tub spout, a shower valve that will not shut off or a showerhead that leaks behind the wall is a fixture repair. A slow-draining tub or shower is a drain problem, which belongs to drain cleaning, and a utility sink or outdoor spigot is handled the same way as a kitchen faucet. A frozen outdoor spigot in winter can crack the pipe behind it, which becomes a burst pipe call.

Remodels and New Fixtures

If you are adding a sink, replacing a vanity or moving a fixture, the supply and drain work falls under plumbing installation. We do the same for kitchens and baths in Westborough and the other towns we serve. A faucet repair visit is also a good time to ask about a new shutoff valve, a new supply line or an aerator, since the cabinet is already open.

Repair or Replace the Faucet?

If the faucet is a few years old and a cartridge will fix it, repair is the answer. If it is decades old, pitted or discontinued, a new faucet costs little more than repeated repairs. We tell you which is better and quote both.

What to Do If a Faucet Will Not Shut Off

Close the stop valves under the sink first. If they will not turn, shut the main valve and call. Do not force a corroded stop with a large wrench, because it can snap and flood the cabinet.

Fixture work is covered by the state plumbing standards in 248 CMR 10.00, which is one reason a plumber handles supply connections.
