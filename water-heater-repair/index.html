Leaking Tank or No Hot Water: What to Do Now

If water is collecting around the heater, close the cold water supply valve on the pipe running into the top of the tank. Turn the electric breaker off, or set a gas heater's control to pilot or off, and then call the 24/7 line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility before you call us.

No hot water is less urgent than a leak but still worth a same-day call in winter. Tell the dispatcher whether the heater is gas or electric, how old it is and whether you hear anything, because those details narrow the cause before we arrive.

What the Symptom Usually Means

Most water heater calls fall into a handful of patterns, and the first step is separating a failed part from a failed tank.

A Leaking Water Heater: Where the Water Really Comes From

Not every puddle means the tank has failed. A loose inlet or outlet fitting, a dripping temperature and pressure relief valve or a condensation drip from a cold tank can all look like a tank leak. A plumber dries the area, checks each joint and traces the source before recommending anything. If the dampness keeps returning with no visible source, leak detection can find a hidden supply leak that only looks like a heater problem.

If the water is coming from the tank body itself, the steel liner has corroded through and no repair will hold. That is the point to move to water heater replacement, ideally before the leak spreads into finished flooring.

Gas, Electric and Hybrid Heaters

Gas units rely on a pilot or electronic igniter, a thermocouple and a gas control valve, and any work on the gas side must be done by a plumber or gas fitter. Electric units use two heating elements and thermostats that fail one at a time, which is why hot water sometimes runs out halfway through a shower.

Hybrid heat pump water heaters add a compressor, fans and a condensate drain, so a service call covers more parts. Tell the dispatcher the model if you know it, and the same applies on calls from Grafton or Northborough. For tankless units, see our tankless water heater page.

Repair or Replace: A Plain Rule of Thumb

Many tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years. If yours is near the end of that range and needs a major part, the repair money often buys a few months. If it is only a few years old, a thermostat or element repair makes sense, and the plumber should say so honestly.

Shrewsbury Water and Your Heater

Shrewsbury's public water comes from groundwater wells, and groundwater can carry dissolved minerals that settle inside a tank. Sediment makes a heater noisier and less efficient and can shorten its life. If you want specific numbers, ask the Shrewsbury Water Department for its latest water quality report or test your tap.

A yearly flush of the tank is a simple way to slow buildup. We are also happy to add an anode rod check to a repair visit, since that sacrificial rod is what protects the steel from corroding.
