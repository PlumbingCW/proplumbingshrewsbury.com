Restaurants and Shops Along Route 9

The Boston Turnpike corridor in Shrewsbury has restaurants, shops and offices side by side, and the lines under them take a lot of use. Grease is the main restaurant problem. It coats pipe walls and hardens, which is why hydro jetting on a schedule keeps kitchens from shutting down. A camera inspection of the building's main line shows whether grease or roots are the cause.

Neighboring Westborough, which sits along the same Route 9 corridor, has a similar mix of commercial buildings. Businesses near the Common can read the Town Center page.

Grease Traps, Backflow Testing and Storm Drains

Grease traps and interceptors need regular pumping and cleaning, and the schedule is often set by the town or the sewer authority, so ask the town what applies to you. Backflow preventers on commercial water lines usually need periodic testing by a certified tester, and your water department can tell you the rules for your building. Parking lot and storm drains clog with leaves and debris and flood a lot when they do.

We handle the plumbing side of each. Specialized systems such as fire sprinklers and medical gas are separate trades with their own licenses, so ask the dispatcher before booking.

Commercial Leak Detection

Leaks in commercial buildings can run for weeks inside walls, above ceilings and under slab floors. Pressure testing, acoustic listening and thermal scans narrow the search, and leak detection covers how each is used. For a building with a long water service line, water line repair may be part of the fix.

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives at a Business

Tell staff which fixtures to stop using, post a sign on affected restrooms and keep customers away from wet floors. Do not pour grease or cleaning chemicals into a backed-up floor drain.
