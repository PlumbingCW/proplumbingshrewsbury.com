What a Sewer Camera Inspection Shows

A sewer inspection sends a waterproof camera on a flexible rod through the cleanout and down the line toward the street. The camera records video as it goes, so you see the pipe from the inside instead of guessing. The plumber watches in real time and marks anything worth a second look.

A good inspection answers three questions. What is the pipe made of, what condition is it in, and where exactly is any problem located? A locator sonde on the camera head lets the plumber mark the surface above a defect.

When an Inspection Is Worth Booking

Most inspections happen for one of these reasons.

  • Before buying a house: the sewer line is out of sight, and a repair can cost more than a kitchen remodel.
  • After repeat clogs: a clog that keeps returning has a cause, and the camera finds it.
  • Before digging or lining: a repair plan built on video avoids opening the wrong spot.
  • When you plan a basement bathroom: new fixtures add load to a line that may already be struggling.
  • After a sewer backup: it confirms whether the pipe is damaged or only blocked.

Older Streets, Older Pipes

Shrewsbury has houses from many eras, and the lateral from an older home may be clay, cast iron or another material that has aged. Pipe material and condition matter when you buy, because a failed line is the owner's responsibility. A video of the line gives a buyer something concrete to bring to the negotiation, whether the house is in Shrewsbury or on the east side of Worcester.

For a homeowner staying put, the camera is the cheapest way to see if a problem is coming. A line that is rough but sound can be maintained, and a cracked one can be repaired before a backup floods the basement.

How the Inspection Works

  1. 1Locate the access point.The plumber finds the cleanout, or removes a toilet if the house has none.
  2. 2Clear obvious debris.If the line is too dirty to see through, a quick round of drain cleaning comes first.
  3. 3Run the camera.The camera runs toward the street, with the plumber watching and recording.
  4. 4Mark and explain.Problem spots are located on the surface, and you get an explanation of what each one means.

If the camera finds roots, sewer line cleaning is the next step. If it finds a break, the repair options are covered on the sewer line repair page.
