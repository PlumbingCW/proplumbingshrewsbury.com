Plumbing for New Fixtures and Remodels

Every new sink, shower or toilet needs a water supply, a drain and a vent, and all three have to meet code before the walls close. Plumbing installation covers new bathrooms, kitchen remodels, laundry hookups, basement baths and fixture upgrades. We work with homeowners, contractors and designers on timing, because plumbing usually comes before drywall and again at the finish.

If you are replacing fixtures only, such as a faucet or toilet, a repair-style visit is often enough, and faucet repair and toilet repair cover those.

Basement and Below-Grade Plumbing

A basement bathroom often sits below the main drain, so waste has to be pumped up. A sewage ejector pit and pump lift it to the house drain, and it needs venting and a proper cover. Lower-level plumbing also needs a plan for water, which is why a bath near a sump pump or a damp slab deserves a look at the basement first.

Permits and Inspections

We handle the permit and schedule the inspections as part of the job.

  • Permit before work: Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR, requires a permit issued by the inspector before most plumbing and gas work starts.
  • Licensed applicant: permits are pulled by a plumber, not by the property owner for a contractor.
  • Rough inspection: the inspector checks pipes before the walls are closed.
  • Final inspection: the inspector signs off on the finished fixtures.
  • Ask the town: the Shrewsbury building department can answer questions about your specific project.

Gas Lines: Safety First

Gas line work for a range, dryer, grill or water heater is plumbing work that needs a permit and a licensed professional. If you ever smell gas, leave the building, call 911 or the gas utility and do not call a plumber first. We install and extend gas lines only with a permit and a pressure test.

A new gas appliance may also need the gas meter and the main gas line sized for the added demand. We check that before we run pipe, and a move to a tankless heater is a common example, covered under tankless water heater installs.

Working With Older Shrewsbury Houses

Opening a wall in an older Shrewsbury house can reveal surprises, such as galvanized supply, old cast iron drains or venting that is no longer to code. Add a few days of flexibility to your schedule and a note in the budget for corrections. The same applies to projects in Westborough, and big surprises sometimes turn a remodel into repiping.

What to Do Before New Fixtures Go In

Confirm the fixture models, clear the room and mark where you want supply and drain lines. Do not cut into walls or floors before the plumber has checked what is behind them.

New work follows the state plumbing standards in 248 CMR 10.00, and your plumber can say whether an inspection is needed.
