Local plumbing, in plain terms

What Shrewsbury Houses Ask of Their Pipes

Shrewsbury was incorporated in 1727 and grew quickly as a streetcar suburb, with the population roughly doubling between 1915 and 1940. That growth left many streets with houses close to a century old. In houses of that age, original galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains are common finds, and both are worth a look when pressure drops or drains keep slowing down.

Homes built in later decades usually carry copper or PEX supply lines and PVC drains. Those materials tend to fail at fittings, shutoff valves and fixtures rather than along the pipe, so a small drip under a sink deserves attention before it becomes a ruined cabinet.

Public water in town comes from groundwater wells run by the Shrewsbury Water Department. Groundwater tends to carry dissolved minerals, which can build up inside water heaters and fixtures over the years. If you want real numbers before sizing a heater or a softener, ask the department for its latest water quality report or test your tap.

Shops and restaurants along Route 9 and around the Town Common need after-hours service too, which is why commercial plumbing gets the same 24/7 dispatch. And if your house sits near Lake Quinsigamond or in a low spot, test the sump pump before heavy rain, not after.

While You Wait for the Plumber

The first ten minutes decide how much damage a leak or backup causes. These four steps are safe for most homeowners and cost nothing.

Stop the waterClose the main shutoff valve, usually where the supply line enters the basement or utility room, then open a low faucet to drain the pipes.
Mind the electricityIf water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, leave them alone and cut power at the breaker only from a dry spot.
Clear and photographMove rugs, boxes and furniture out of the wet area, then photograph the damage and the source for your insurer.
Keep the line freeStay off drains and toilets during a backup, and keep your phone nearby so the 24/7 dispatcher can reach you.
