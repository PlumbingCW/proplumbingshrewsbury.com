How to Clean a Clogged Drain: Shrewsbury, MA Homeowner Guide

By the Shrewsbury Pro Plumbing teamShrewsbury, MA homeowner guide

Most slow drains clear with a few simple steps and a bit of patience. This guide shows what works, what to avoid and the point where a plumber with the right equipment saves you time.

Call (508) 389-4045

Start With the Cause

A clog in one fixture is usually local, such as hair in a tub or grease in a kitchen sink. If several fixtures slow down together, the blockage is in a branch or the main drain. That difference decides how far you should go on your own.

Water backing up in a basement floor drain or a lower-level tub when you run the washer points to the main line. Stop using water and call, because a DIY snake is unlikely to reach it.

Clearing a Sink or Tub

These steps solve most single-fixture clogs without chemicals.

  1. 1Remove what you can seeLift out the stopper and pull hair and debris from the opening.
  2. 2Use a plunger the right wayBlock the overflow with a wet rag, cover the drain with water and plunge with firm, steady strokes.
  3. 3Try a hand drain snakeFeed it in, turn the handle as it meets resistance and pull the clog out in pieces.
  4. 4Flush with hot waterRun it for a minute afterward to carry loosened material down the line.
  5. 5Clean the trap if neededPlace a bucket under the P-trap, unscrew it and clear it by hand.

Why Chemical Cleaners Are a Poor Bet

Store-bought drain cleaners are caustic. They can sit in a pipe that is fully blocked and damage it, and they are hazardous to anyone who then has to work on the drain. If a plumber has to open a pipe full of cleaner, the job becomes more dangerous for them.

A snake or a plunger does the same job without the risk. If you have already poured a cleaner in, tell the plumber when you call.

Deciding early saves the cost of making a small clog worse.

Kitchen Versus Bathroom: Different Clogs, Different Fixes

Bathroom clogs are mostly hair and soap scum, and they sit near the opening where a snake or a pulled stopper reaches them. Kitchen clogs are mostly grease and food that hardened further down the line. The kitchen blockage tends to be longer and more stubborn, so a quick plunge often fails.

Hot water helps with a grease clog only briefly, since the grease re-sets further along. Jetting is the lasting answer for a kitchen line that fills up every few weeks.

Habits That Keep Drains Clear

Fit a mesh strainer over tub and shower drains and empty it after each use. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing, and never pour cooking fat down the sink. Run hot water for a minute after the dishes.

Flush only toilet paper, and keep wipes and cotton products out of the toilet, even when the package says flushable. A few small habits cost nothing and prevent most calls.

Floor Drains, Washers and Laundry Lines

A basement floor drain that gurgles or holds water is telling you something. It often means the line beyond it is partly blocked or that the trap has dried out. Pour a quart of water down it and watch how fast it clears.

Washing machine drains are narrow and clog with lint. A mesh sock on the discharge hose catches most of it, and a standpipe that overflows during a wash is a sign the line needs cleaning.

Older Shrewsbury Drains

Many Shrewsbury houses date from the streetcar suburb era, and a lot of them still have original cast iron or clay drain lines under the basement floor. Those materials roughen and scale over the decades, which gives hair and grease something to grab.

If you own an older home and your drains slow repeatedly, a camera look is cheaper than guessing. Ask the town about sewer connection rules if the backup reaches the street side of your line.
