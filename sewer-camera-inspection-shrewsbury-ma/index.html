CCTV Camera Sewer Pipe Inspection for Shrewsbury, MA Homes

By the Shrewsbury Pro Plumbing teamShrewsbury, MA homeowner guide

A sewer camera turns a guess into a picture. This guide explains what the inspection finds, when it makes the most sense and how to read the report you get back.

Call (508) 389-4045

What a Sewer Camera Actually Does

A sewer inspection uses a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable that runs through the drain line. A light on the camera head shows the pipe wall, and a monitor shows the video as the cable advances. Many systems also carry a transmitter, so the technician can locate the camera above ground and mark the exact spot of a problem.

The result is a recording you can watch and keep. It shows cracks, roots, offsets, sags and buildup without any digging.

What the Camera Can Reveal

Different findings lead to very different fixes, which is why the picture comes first.

When an Inspection Is Worth It

The strongest case is a repeat backup. If a drain clears and then clogs again in a few weeks, something in the pipe is catching debris. Cleaning without looking is a gamble that costs money each time.

The next case is a home purchase. Buyers of older houses use a camera to check the lateral before closing, so they know what they are taking on. A third case is planning a basement bathroom or a renovation that adds load to the line.

How the Visit Goes

The technician finds a cleanout or removes a toilet to get access, then feeds the camera down the line. They watch the monitor, note distances and mark problem spots. When the run is done, they review what they saw with you and, in most cases, give you a copy of the recording.

A good report ties each finding to a distance from the access point. That lets a repair crew know exactly where to work, which keeps a dig small.

Reading the Report You Get

A useful inspection report lists the pipe material, the footage of the run and each defect with its distance from the access point. It separates minor findings, such as light scale, from serious ones, such as a collapsed section. It should also say where the camera stopped and why.

If the camera could not pass a blockage or a broken section, the report should say so. A partial run is still useful, but you should know that part of the line was not seen.

What Roots Tell You

Roots enter pipes at joints, looking for moisture and nutrients. Fine roots show as white hair, while older growth forms a thick mass that catches paper and waste. A tree near the line, a sewer that clogs every spring and a camera view of root hair at each joint all point the same way.

Cutting roots clears the line for a time, but they return. If the joints are open, a lining or a repair can seal them so the roots have nothing to enter.

Sewers in Shrewsbury

Shrewsbury's older streets were built up quickly in the early twentieth century, and homes from that period often have clay or cast iron laterals, the pipes a camera most often finds in trouble. In many towns the lateral from the house to the main is the owner's responsibility.

The sewer rules and connection records for your street are held by the town. Ask Shrewsbury's public works or sewer office about your connection before you plan a repair that reaches the property line.

What Comes After the Camera

A clean run needs nothing more than a note in your home file. A grease problem leads to hydro jetting, and a damaged section leads to a repair estimate. Our sewer line repair page explains spot repair, relining and replacement.

Ask the technician to show you the footage before you agree to work. A finding you have seen yourself is easier to trust and easier to compare with a second opinion.

Cameras for Buyers and Sellers

A seller can order an inspection before listing, so a surprise does not derail the sale. A buyer can order one during the inspection period, and the recording gives both sides a shared reference. If the camera finds a problem, the repair cost becomes part of the negotiation.

Keep the recording with the property records. A later owner, an insurer or a plumber will find it useful.
