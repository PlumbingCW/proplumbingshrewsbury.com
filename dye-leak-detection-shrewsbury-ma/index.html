Dye for Leak Detection: Shrewsbury, MA Homeowner Guide

By the Shrewsbury Pro Plumbing teamShrewsbury, MA homeowner guide

Food coloring in the toilet tank is the simplest leak test there is. This guide shows how to do it, how to read the result and when you need tools that see through walls.

Call (508) 389-4045

Why Dye Works for Toilet Leaks

A worn flapper lets water slip from the tank into the bowl without a flush. The water refills the tank in small amounts, and the leak is silent. Dye in the tank shows up in the bowl if the flapper is leaking.

A toilet leak can waste a lot of water over a month. Because it is quiet, many people only notice when the water bill climbs.

How to Run the Dye Test

The test takes about twenty minutes and costs next to nothing.

  1. 1Remove the tank lidSet it somewhere safe and look at the water level.
  2. 2Add the dyeDrop in a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet, enough to color the tank water.
  3. 3Wait without flushingGive it fifteen to twenty minutes, and keep everyone from using that toilet.
  4. 4Check the bowlIf color appears in the bowl, the flapper or flush valve seat is leaking.
  5. 5Flush and clean upFlush to clear the dye. Stained tank parts are harmless but may mark the porcelain.

Dye Test Results and Next Steps

A negative result matters too, since it points you to the next suspect.

ResultMeaningFix
Color in the bowlFlapper or seat leakingReplace the flapper, clean the seat
No color, but the toilet runsFill valve or float issueAdjust or replace the fill valve
No color, tank level dropsOverflow tube leak or a crackCheck the overflow and tank
Clear bowl, water bill still highLeak elsewhereCheck the meter, then call for detection

What Dye Cannot Find

Dye tests toilets. It does not locate a leak in a wall, under a slab or in a buried supply line. For those, a plumber uses acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging and pressure testing to narrow the area before opening anything.

Our leak detection page explains these tools. For a leak in the line between the street and your house, water service line repair covers the fix.

The Meter Test

Shut off every fixture, then read the water meter and check that the leak indicator is still. If the dial or indicator keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere in the house or the service line. Note the reading, wait an hour with nothing running, and read it again.

A change in that hour is a confirmed leak. A plumber can then work out whether it is inside the house or in the buried line.

Other Silent Leaks Worth Checking

Toilets are the most common silent leak, but they are not the only one. Check under sinks for damp cabinets, around the water heater for rust or moisture and at outdoor spigots for drips. A garden hose left connected can also hold a slow leak at the bib.

Look at the ceiling below a bathroom and the wall behind a washing machine. A brown ring, a soft spot or paint bubbling away from the surface is worth following up.

Using the Meter for the Bigger Picture

If the dye test is clean and the meter still runs with everything off, the leak is in a pipe. It may be inside a wall or in the buried line from the street. The next step is to isolate the house from the service line by closing the main valve and watching the meter again.

If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the buried service line, and water service line repair is likely the fix.

Fixing the Flapper Once the Dye Shows It

A flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. Shut off the toilet's supply valve, flush to empty the tank and unhook the old flapper from its pegs. Take it to a supplier or match the model printed on the tank, then clip the new one in and adjust the chain so it has a little slack.

Turn the water back on and let the tank refill. Run the dye test again after the repair, since a flapper that seats well keeps the bowl clear.

Seasonal Leaks to Watch

Outdoor spigots can freeze and split in a cold snap, and the leak only shows when the water is turned back on in spring. Check the inside wall behind a hose bib for dampness after the first use. A frost-proof spigot with a hose still attached can fail the same way.

Disconnect hoses before the first freeze and shut off interior stops to outdoor lines if your house has them.

Water Bills and Leaks in Shrewsbury

Water in Shrewsbury comes from the town's groundwater wells and is billed through the town water department. A bill that jumps with no change in habits is worth checking against your meter.

Ask the water department how often your meter is read and whether they offer an alert for unusual use. They can tell you what your account shows, and the answer may save you a search.
