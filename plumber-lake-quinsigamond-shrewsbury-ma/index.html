The Shrewsbury Shore at a Glance

Every item above comes from a state or town source. None of it says what is inside any one house.

  • The lake: a four mile, bow-shaped lake that forms the boundary between Worcester and Shrewsbury, per the Massachusetts Historical Commission reconnaissance report.
  • Shoreline history: the same report records summer resort growth, a streetcar branch down the east side of the lake and a hotel, a park and boat shops along the shore.
  • Housing: the report describes lakeside cottages, clubs and recreational areas along South Quinsigamond Avenue, and modest bungalow-type cottages near the lake at the edge of the Fairlawn development.
  • Sewer work: a 2020 town project added new sewer mains on Lake Street and Route 20 and upgraded pump stations, including one at Route 20 and Lakeside Drive.
  • Boundary: the water line is clear, but no official neighborhood boundary exists for the shore. This page uses the lake and its anchor streets.

Waterfront Homes Have Their Own Questions

Cottages that began as summer places and homes that have been lived in year round can sit side by side on a lake shore. Owners often ask about sump pumps, drains that slow in wet weather and water heaters in tight utility spaces.

Those are general questions, not findings about this shore. See sump pump service, drain cleaning and water heater repair for what each visit covers. Plumbing work in Massachusetts falls under the state plumbing standards in 248 CMR 10.00.

Water Tables and Sump Pumps: What Is General and What Is Not

Land near any lake or pond may sit close to the water table, which is one reason some owners keep a sump pump. A pump that never runs until it is needed can fail without warning, so testing it matters more than its age.

We have no groundwater data for specific Shrewsbury addresses, so we do not say which lakeside basements are wet. If you see water, a plumber can check the pit, the pump and the discharge line and explain what the equipment is doing.
