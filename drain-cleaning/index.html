When a Clogged Drain Becomes an Emergency

A slow bathroom sink is a nuisance. Water rising in the tub when you flush, or sewage showing in a basement floor drain, is an emergency, and our 24/7 line takes those calls at any hour in Shrewsbury. While you wait, stop running water, leave the toilets unflushed and pull rugs and boxes back from the drain.

Every fixture empties into a branch line, and the branches feed one main. Which drains back up tells us where the clog sits. If only the kitchen sink is stuck, the blockage is local. If the tub gurgles whenever the washer drains, it is farther down and often in the main.

Reading the Symptoms Before We Arrive

The notes you give the dispatcher shape the visit. These are the patterns plumbers listen for.

  • One fixture slow or stopped: a clog in the trap or the branch serving it, usually cleared from the fixture or a nearby cleanout.
  • Tub and toilet back up together: the blockage is past the point where those branches join, which points toward the main line.
  • Gurgling when the washing machine drains: air is being pushed through a partly blocked line, an early warning before a full backup.
  • Standing water or odor at a floor drain: the floor drain sits low, so it is the first place a main-line problem shows.
  • The same drain clogs every few weeks: scale, grease or roots are re-forming, and a camera look is cheaper than the fifth snaking.

A repeat clog usually deserves a look inside the pipe, which is what a sewer camera inspection shows.

How We Clear a Drain

The method follows the clog. A hand auger handles a hair-and-soap trap clog in a sink or tub. A cable machine with a cutter head goes through roots, scale and compacted debris in a 3 or 4 inch line. When grease has coated the pipe wall, hydro jetting scours it clean where a cable only punches a hole through it.

We start from the nearest cleanout when there is one, because it avoids pulling a toilet. If the line is still slow afterward, we measure how far the cable ran and where it hit resistance. That distance tells you whether the problem sits under the house or out toward the street.

Kitchen Sink Clogs Are Their Own Problem

Kitchen lines clog with cooking grease, coffee grounds and starchy food that cools into a paste inside the pipe. The clog often forms beyond the trap, so plunging pushes it deeper instead of clearing it. A disposal that hums but drains slowly points the same way, and a jammed unit is a separate fix covered under garbage disposal repair.

Keep hot water running while the plumber works and avoid chemical openers beforehand. Those products sit in the pipe, can damage older fittings and put caustic liquid in front of whoever opens the trap next.
