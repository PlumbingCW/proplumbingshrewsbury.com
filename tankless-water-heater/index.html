What a Tankless Heater Changes in Your House

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to run out and no standby loss. The trade-off is that the unit needs a large burst of gas or electricity, a proper vent and a minimum flow before it fires. A house with a small gas line or old venting often needs upgrades before the unit can work.

Condensing models extract extra heat from the exhaust and vent with plastic pipe, which can be easier to route than a metal flue. They also produce acidic condensate that must be drained safely.

Flushing and Descaling Your Tankless Heater

Minerals in the water coat the heat exchanger over time, which cuts flow and triggers error codes. Manufacturers commonly recommend a flush at least once a year, and more often in hard water. We circulate a descaling solution through the unit, rinse it and check the filter screens.

Shrewsbury's public water is groundwater, which tends to carry minerals, so a regular flush is cheap protection. If you want actual figures, ask the Shrewsbury Water Department for its latest water quality report or test your tap.

Fitting a Tankless Heater Into an Older House

A tankless heater draws far more gas at once than a tank, so a house with a small gas line or a long run from the meter may need the line upsized. Venting is the other hurdle, because the exhaust has to reach the outside through a wall or the roof. Older Shrewsbury houses sometimes have a chimney flue that cannot be reused.

Those upgrades are plumbing work, covered under plumbing installation, and they are the reason a tankless quote can look high next to a tank. A planned look at the gas line and vent path before you buy avoids a surprise. Homeowners in Northborough face the same questions.

When a Tank Is the Better Choice

A tank suits a small household, a house with a weak gas supply or a budget that cannot absorb venting work. It also has fewer parts to scale up. If you are undecided, we price both and let you compare, and tank water heater installation is a quick job when the existing setup is sound.

Tankless Problems We Fix

Most calls fall into a few patterns.

  • Cold water sandwich: hot water goes cold, then hot again, often from low flow or a scaled exchanger.
  • Error codes on the display: ignition, flame, vent and flow faults each point to a different part.
  • Low flow or weak output: scale or a clogged inlet filter is the usual cause.
  • Unit will not ignite: gas supply, sensor or igniter problems.
  • Condensate drain backup: a blocked drain trips condensing models.

If the unit is past repair, we explain replacement options, including a return to a tank under water heater replacement.

What to Do If a Tankless Unit Shows an Error Code

Write down the code, check that the gas and water valves are open and do not reset the unit repeatedly. Do not bypass safety sensors or vent parts.

Installation and venting follow the manufacturer's rules and the state plumbing standards in 248 CMR 10.00.
