Mill-Era Homes and Older Streets: What Is Behind Grafton's Walls

Grafton was incorporated in 1735, and parts of it grew around mills, including the Washington Mills complex and the mill housing on streets like Overlook Street. Houses of that era often have long cast iron drains, original galvanized supply and tight basements. Those pipes are worth checking when drains slow or pressure sags.

If you own an older home in Fisherville, Saundersville or Farnumsville, expect to see mixed materials where earlier owners patched the original. A repiping assessment shows whether the house still has working life in its supply lines.

Grafton's Wells and Water Chemistry

The Grafton Water District pumps from wells on East Street in North Grafton, on Worcester Street and elsewhere, and it adjusts the pH of the water and disinfects it before delivery. The aquifer is rated highly vulnerable, so the district watches it closely. A separate South Grafton Water District serves part of town, so ask which district you are in if you want a water quality report.

Treated groundwater still carries minerals that settle in water heaters over time. Newer homes with PEX and PVC handle that better than old steel, and water heater repair often starts with a sediment flush.
