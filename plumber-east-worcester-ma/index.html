Lake Quinsigamond is about four miles long and separates Shrewsbury from Worcester. The Route 9 bridge, rebuilt and reopened in 2015, links the two sides, and it is how a plumber reaches the east side. Quinsigamond Village, Shrewsbury Street and the Commercial Strip

Worcester's Shrewsbury Street is known for restaurants, and kitchens that busy need grease lines kept clear. Hydro jetting on a schedule is how many of them avoid a backup on a weekend night. Quinsigamond Village and the streets by the lake have older houses and multi-family buildings, where a shared drain stack can back up into more than one unit.

For those buildings, sewer line cleaning and a camera inspection are the usual pair, and landlords calling for tenants are welcome.

Worcester Water: Soft, Treated and Reservoir-Fed

Worcester's Department of Public Works reports water hardness of about 30 ppm, with a range of roughly 22 to 55 across the system, which is soft by common classifications. The city treats the water with a phosphate corrosion inhibitor and adjusts pH with lime. Soft water builds scale slowly, so heaters on the east side tend to fail from age and corrosion more than from mineral buildup.

That makes the age of the tank and its anode rod the best clues, which is a question for water heater repair visits.
